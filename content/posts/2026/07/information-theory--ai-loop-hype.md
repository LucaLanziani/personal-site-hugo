There is a hard limit to what autonomous AI "loops" can achieve from a single, high-level goal.

I know, I know. That is not the most exciting sentence to write in the middle of an AI hype cycle.

But I think it matters, because a lot of the current agent conversation seems to assume that you can ask for a city while only providing the blueprints for a shed.

<!--more-->

## We Already Trust People This Way

Can you trust an AI model to assume some missing pieces?

Of course. That is not weird. That is exactly what we do when we hire human professionals.

You don't tell them every step. You give them the goal and trust their judgment.

But look at how that scales.

If your hot water isn't running, you don't tell the plumber how to fix it. You just say, "the water is not watering." They iterate, troubleshoot, and figure it out.

For standard, familiar work, this is fine.

If you tell a carpenter, "Build me a shed," they can still make reasonable assumptions. Standard dimensions. Standard materials. Standard constraints.

If you tell a developer, "Build me a condo," they need more input. There are still templates and conventions, but the number of important decisions starts to rise.

If you tell an architect, "Build me a skyscraper," they don't just start pouring concrete. They come back with 500 questions about zoning, budget, wind loads, elevators, materials, and load-bearing structures.

And if you tell an urban planner, "Build me a city," the goal is almost meaningless without a master plan for power, traffic, housing, incentives, regulations, and social dynamics.

Easy peasy, "Just build the city."

## Complexity Needs Information

The professional will figure out the work, but as complexity rises, they know they cannot build your specific vision without extracting the specific details from you.

This is where the current AI hype hits the brick wall of Information Theory.

You cannot compress specific intent. You cannot describe a highly complex system without actually describing it in detail.

Right now, we are typing one-sentence goals into AI loops and expecting fully functional digital cities on the other end.

And when the AI doesn't stop to ask the 500 questions, it starts pouring digital concrete based on generic assumptions.

That can still be useful. I use these tools every day, and I don't want to pretend they are just autocomplete with a nicer jacket.

But they do not remove the need for domain knowledge. They make the missing pieces visible faster.

## What are you looping on?

AI doesn't replace the need for detailed architecture, clear requirements, and people who understand the problem space.

So the real question is: are you asking your AI to fix a pipe, build a shed, or somehow guess the master plan for a city?

And if you are going for more than a shed, are you prepared to answer the 500 questions?
